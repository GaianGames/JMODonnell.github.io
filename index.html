Founding the Collective

When I arrived in Galway in 2019, a prose collective didn't exist, and I suffered under the common belief that writing is something that has to be done in isolation. However, I wanted to change this, and when I couldn't find a collective, I decided to form one.

The Galway Writing Collective was born out of a desire to give a space to prose writers and to develop a sense of community. I wanted to help foster a sense of connection and nurture the arts in Galway while bringing people together.

  • A weekly prose workshop to nurture and support writers of various levels.
  • Crafted a brand and built a public following including literary journals, publishers, and authors.
  • Facilitated four monthly events, liaised with venues, and oversaw event management.
  • Ran Galway’s only regular technical editing workshop once a month.

The group is now on hiatus, but discussions are underway to return in a more sustainable way.

J.M O'Donnell, Founder of The Galway Writing Collective.

There he developed key legacy systems such as the Honoring and the Arrill Rift."

"As it is difficult to find stand-out talent for creative roles, we had plenty of demand for John's capabilities in other sectors. He gave meaningful feedback on storylines for our upcoming movies and comic scripts as well as for the overall story structure of our upcoming seasons. During the process, his thinking was at all times well balanced between the principles of story structure which he knows incredibly well, and a creative sensibility allowing for new insights that were surprising and fresh."

"Even though John does not work with us at this point on a regular basis in favor of his current work, we always enjoy the moments when we see him return — if only for a brief project or editing session — bringing valuable feedback every single time."

"Easily one of the most talented, dedicated, professional, and unique writers I know, John is a massive asset to any project he is attached to. In the demand-constrained entertainment industry where quantity is an inadequate substitute for quality, bringing someone like him on board can certainly be the difference between failure and success."
